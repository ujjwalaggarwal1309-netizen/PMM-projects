# I analyzed 5 project management tools - here is who is winning the positioning battle and why

**Published on Medium | By Ujjwal | September 2026**

---

Linear has fewer features than Asana. Its pricing page is simpler. Its marketing budget is a fraction of Monday.com's - they spent an estimated \$35,000 total on paid ads in their first years.

And yet engineering teams are leaving Asana and Jira for Linear at a rate that nobody at those companies seems to want to talk about publicly.

One Reddit thread compared Linear's API response time to Jira's: 47ms vs. 3,200ms.

I spent 3 weeks analyzing how five competing project management tools - Notion, Asana, Monday.com, Linear, and Trello - are actually positioned in the market. I read 75+ G2 reviews, 20+ Reddit threads, audited every homepage and pricing page, and pulled 100+ verbatim data points.

---

## The strongest positioning belongs to the tool you might not have heard of

Linear wins the positioning battle - not because it has the most features, but because it is the only tool in this category that deliberately excludes customers.

Their homepage reads: *"The product development system for teams and agents."* Not "for any team." Not "for every workflow." For *product development teams*. That specificity is a strategic choice.

They even deliberated the word "system" over "tool" - infrastructure-level framing that signals they want to be the operating layer of how software gets built, not just another SaaS subscription.

The reviews confirm it. One engineering manager on G2 wrote: *"Linear ships with strong opinions: cycles auto-roll, statuses are pre-defined, priority is a 4-level enum. Teams adopt the Linear way rather than configuring custom workflows."*

That is what sharp positioning produces: users who describe the product as *intentional*. Not useful. Not powerful. Intentional. Another G2 reviewer, a Director of Product, put it this way: *"It's like someone took Jira, threw out everything that frustrated me about it, and rebuilt it from scratch with a 2024 sensibility."*

---

## The biggest gap between promise and reality belongs to Monday.com

Monday.com's homepage tells you they offer *"clarity and control at scale."* Their customers tell a different story.

From a marketing director on G2: *"I feel like every quarter they add 3 new products and remove something I actually use. I signed up for a project management tool and now I'm being sold a CRM, an IT service desk, and a dev platform. I don't need any of that."*

From an operations manager on Reddit: *"Billing is a nightmare. They locked us into an annual contract, then added features to a higher tier without telling us. When we called support, we waited 4 days for a response."*

Monday.com sells "the AI Work Platform for People & Agents" - a claim so broad it could mean anything to anyone.

They are the most visible brand in the category and the least trusted by their own customers. That gap is not a PR problem.

---

## The hidden story: Trello's quiet death as a team tool

In May 2025, Atlassian made a decision that most people outside the PM tools space missed: they announced they would no longer accept feature requests for team-based project management in Trello. Overnight, a product used by 50+ million people was officially repositioned as a personal productivity companion.

The Reddit verdict was immediate and brutal: *"Congratulations, Trello is now hot garbage."* Another user started a viral thread: *"Atlassian ruined Trello so I built an open source alternative."*

This matters for one specific reason: teams leaving Trello are not going to Linear. They're going to ClickUp, Asana, GitHub Projects, and niche tools like Superthread and Huly. No major player has positioned themselves explicitly for "the team that outgrew Trello."

---

## The white space no one in this category is filling

Here is the insight that surprised me most: every tool in this category is horizontal.

Notion is for "every team." Asana is for "any workflow." Monday.com is for "every department." Trello is for "anyone who needs a board." Even Linear - the most focused - is horizontal within engineering.

No one has gone vertical by department type.

Search Reddit for *"project management tool for marketing teams"* and you will find it is one of the most common questions in r/projectmanagement. The answer is always a workaround: *"Use Asana and customize it,"* or *"Try Notion with these templates."* Nobody says: *"This tool was designed for how your team actually works."*

Marketing teams, legal teams, creative teams, and operations teams are being handed engineering tools and told to adapt.

The team that builds and positions a PM tool specifically for how a marketing campaign moves from brief to launch - not as templates, but as the default product workflow - will own that segment the way Linear owns engineering.

---

## The one thing I would change if I were a PMM at any of these companies

If I worked at Asana, I would stop the AI Teammates campaign and go back to basics: own "accountability" as a positioning word the way no one else does. Every competitor is now claiming AI. No one is claiming that they are the tool that makes accountability possible inside a growing company. That is Asana's original strength - and they are abandoning it to chase a trend.

The market does not need another AI work platform. It needs someone to make accountability feel possible again as teams grow past 50 people.

---

## What this tells you

Sharp positioning is not about saying more. It is about having the discipline to say less - and meaning it. Linear says it and means it. Notion, Asana, Monday.com, and Trello are still figuring it out.

The next winner in this category will not have more features. They will have a clearer answer to a specific person's specific problem.

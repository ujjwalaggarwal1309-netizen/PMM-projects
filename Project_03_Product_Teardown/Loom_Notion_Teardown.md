> ðŸ–¼ï¸ *[Insert screenshot of Loom's Welcome Email]*

**The PMM Analysis:**
The onboarding sequence is ruthlessly optimized for one behavior. 
* **Email 1:** Ignores 95% of features. The only CTA is a massive button: "Start Recording." Loom knows their "aha!" moment isn't configuring settings; it's the moment you save yourself a 30-minute Zoom call.
* **Email 2:** Focuses on sharing. A video is useless if nobody watches it. Loom uses the dopamine hit of the "viewed" notification to drive retention.

## ðŸ’° 4. Pricing: The PLG Engine

> ðŸ–¼ï¸ *[Insert screenshot of Loom's Pricing Page]*

**The PMM Analysis:**
The free tier allows unlimited team members (driving viral internal growth) but imposes a hard cap of 25 videos and a 5-minute limit per video.
* **Why it works:** It lets teams build the habit for free. By the time they hit the 25-video ceiling, the alternative (going back to long emails) is too painful. Friction to upgrade is minimal.
* **The AI Gate:** Post-Atlassian acquisition, all major AI features (auto-titles, silence trimming) are strictly gated behind the Business tier, making AI their primary margin-driver.

## â­ 5. What They Do Brilliantly

> ðŸ’¡ **The 5-Minute Cap Strategy**
> Users constantly complain on Reddit about the 5-minute limit on free videos. But from a PMM perspective, it is absolute genius. 
> 
> **1. It forces the right behavior:** It trains users to be concise. If free users could record 45-minute rambling videos, the recipients would hate the product. 
> **2. It qualifies leads:** If a user consistently hits the 5-minute mark, they are doing deep presentations. That frustration is the exact friction required to pull out a corporate credit card.
